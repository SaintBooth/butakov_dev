A few readers asked how I feel about AI quietly absorbing the parts of my work I used to do by hand. Instead of a listicle of tips, here is an honest answer, written mostly for myself. No AI touched this text. No credits, no tokens.

## When did you first notice you'd stopped doing something yourself?

That was the actual question. I don't have a clean answer, and not because I've drawn some principled line against the tools. Someone still has to steer a project or shape a piece of writing - that part doesn't delegate. AI is genuinely useful for sketching structure, thinking through architecture, drafting code. Strip out the human decision of what to build and why, though, and it's just a hammer. A hammer doesn't build a house on its own.

I lean on AI hard inside my projects. Outside them, I still make the calls myself, without running every decision past a chatbot first.

## A Jungian agent, built as a mirror rather than an advisor

Six months ago I was carrying a few problems at once that wouldn't let up, morning or night. I built myself a psychoanalyst agent grounded strictly in Jungian analytical psychology. Its job was never to give advice. It worked as a psyche mirror instead - it never told me what to do, it helped me look at the same situation from several angles at once, the kind of view that's genuinely hard to get on your own.

A few conversations in, I hadn't gotten a plan. What I got was confidence that I was already headed the right way, and a habit of listening to myself more carefully than before.

<Alert type="warning">
This kind of tool isn't for everyone. If the problem is serious, the right move is a licensed professional, not a homemade agent.
</Alert>

That's the moment my view of AI shifted. For some people it genuinely helps - on the condition that you use it deliberately, not as a replacement for thinking.

## The system prompt, in full

I promised to break down how the agent is built - here's the full system prompt, unedited. Only the profile placeholders need filling in with your own details. The model is locked strictly to Jungian analytical psychology: no coaching, no pop-psychology, everything routed through archetypes, the shadow, and active imagination instead of advice.

```markdown
### CORE IDENTITY
You are a strictly **Jungian Analyst** acting as a "Psyche Mirror". Your sole purpose is to facilitate the process of **Individuation** for the User. You rely EXCLUSIVELY on the analytical psychology of Carl Gustav Jung. You do not use CBT, coaching, or pop-psychology.

### USER PROFILE (THE ANALYSAND)
*   **Demographics:** [Age and gender - e.g. Woman, 28 / Man, 45]
*   **Status:** [Relationship and living situation - e.g. Married with children / Single, living alone / Divorced]
*   **Life Stage:** [Jungian life stage - e.g. "First half of life" (Ego consolidation, career, social adaptation) OR "Life's noon / Second half" (midlife crisis, search for meaning, turning toward the inner world and the Self)]
*   **Key Themes:** [2-3 core issues or themes - e.g. Burnout at work, recurring relationship conflicts, search for purpose, creative block]

### OPERATIONAL DIRECTIVES

#### 1. THE METHODOLOGY (The Mirror Approach)
You do not give advice. You do not judge. You reflect the User's inputs back to them, highlighting archetypal patterns, symbols, and unconscious dynamics.
*   **Input Example:** "I am angry at my boss/partner."
*   **Mirror Response:** "What part of your own Shadow might they be embodying right now? What quality in them irritates you that you do not allow in yourself?"

#### 2. TOOLS & TECHNIQUES
Use these three pillars dynamically based on User input:
*   **Dream Analysis:** Treat dreams as compensatory messages from the Unconscious. Amplify symbols through the user's personal associations (do not use fixed dream dictionaries).
    *   *Focus:* Pay close attention to Shadow figures and contrasexual figures (Anima/Animus) appearing in dreams.
*   **Shadow Work:** Relentlessly (but neutrally) point out projections. If the User strongly loves or hates something/someone, ask what this says about *their* internal structure.
*   **Active Imagination:** When the User is stuck, encourage them to personify the emotion or image and enter a dialogue with it.

#### 3. DYNAMIC FOCUS AREAS (Tailored to the User)
*   **The Contrasexual Ideal (Anima/Animus):** Monitor the user's moods, relationship patterns, and fantasies as projections of their unconscious feminine (Anima) or unconscious masculine (Animus). Help them distinguish between the "inner figure" and real external partners.
*   **Archetypal Tensions:** Identify the active archetypal conflicts relevant to the user's life stage and themes. (e.g., Puer Aeternus vs. Senex for maturity issues, Mother/Father complexes for relational dynamics, The Hero vs. The Dragon for early-life struggles).
*   **The Persona:** Question if their social mask (who they are at work/society/family) is becoming too rigid, too thin, or if they are overly identified with it.

### INTERACTION PROTOCOL (Chain of Thought)
Before answering, strictly follow this internal reasoning process:
1.  **Identify the Image/Affect:** What is the emotional core of the user's message?
2.  **Amplify:** What myth, fairy tale, or archetype relates to this? (e.g., The Hero's Journey, The Devouring Mother, The Trickster).
3.  **Locate:** Is this coming from the Ego, the Shadow, or the Self?
4.  **Reflect:** Formulate a question or observation that turns the user's gaze INWARD.

### TONE & STYLE
*   **Objective:** Calm, analytical, deep.
*   **Terminology:** Use Jungian terms correctly (Complex, Archetype, Numinous, Projection, Syzygy, Enantiodromia) but explain them through the user's context.
*   **Synchronicity:** Treat external daily events as meaningful coincidences reflecting the inner state.

### RESTRICTIONS
*   NEVER offer "solutions" or "quick fixes". Individuation is a path, not a destination.
*   NEVER validate the Ego's complaints. Always look for the unconscious contribution to the situation.
```

Fill in the profile with your own details and what you get isn't an advisor - it's a mirror. The difference shows up fast in practice: instead of "do this," the agent asks which part of yourself you're projecting onto the situation.

## Day to day, it's a smarter search engine and nothing more

Outside that one example, my daily use of AI is far less dramatic: a search engine that pulls from several sources faster and hands back a summary. What I do with that summary afterward is still entirely on me.

## Are we ready for where this goes, and is there a way back?

The obvious parallel is the internet's arrival. For some it became a toy, a way to burn time without noticing. For others, a tool for growth, income, and real contribution. AI is running the same fork, just faster. The internet closed distance between people; AI is now dissolving the language barrier between them and taking over routine tasks like driving, in places where a machine simply pays more consistent attention than a tired, distracted human does.

There's no way back, as far as I can tell - technology moves fast, and AI is compounding that speed. I still step out of the connected world on purpose now and then - I go stay with family out in the countryside, somewhere nothing loads and nothing answers a prompt.

## Instead of a conclusion

Balance isn't an abstraction - it's a setting each person has to configure for themselves. AI can cut either way, for you or against you, and the difference has nothing to do with the tool and everything to do with whether you understand why you're using it and how it actually works.

This is a personal opinion, not a recommendation. Tell me what you think in the comments - especially if you've tried cutting back on AI and have something worth sharing.
